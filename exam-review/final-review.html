Part 1 - Employment

Suppose a large university wants to know how working > 5 hours a week relates to students’ academic performance.

They randomly sample 50 students; the determine both their working status (more or less than 5 hours a week) and their GPA. They want to test if the GPAs of students working more than 5 hours a week are, on average, the same as the GPAs of students working less than 5 hours a week.

Throughout this part, we will refer to work as the variable equal to Less or More than 5 hours (where Less is the baseline) and gpa as the GPA variable. The data frame is named students.

Question 1

First, suppose we want to do some EDA to examine the distributions of GPAs across the working status categories.

Which of the following types of plots would be most appropriate for doing so? Select all that apply.

  1. Boxplots

  2. Bar chart

  3. Histograms

  4. Pie chart

  5. Density plots

Question 2

Now, suppose we wanted to look at the mean GPA in each category. Which of the following pieces of code could we have run to get the table below?

# A tibble: 2 × 2
  work   mean
  <chr> <dbl>
1 Less   3.58
2 More   3.66
students |> 
  group_by(work) |>
  mutate(mean = mean(gpa))
students |> 
  group_by(gpa) |>
  mutate(mean = mean(gpa))
students |> 
  group_by(work) |>
  summarise(mean = mean(gpa))
students |> 
  summarise(mean = mean(gpa))
students |> 
  mutate(mean = mean(gpa))

Question 3

linear_reg() |>

  fit(gpa ~ work, data = students)
logistic_reg() |>
  fit(work ~ students, data = students)
students |>
  specify(gpa ~ work) |>
  fit()
students |>
  specify(work ~ gpa) |>
  fit()

  1. \(\widehat{gpa} = b_0 + b_1\times work\)
  2. \(\widehat{gpa} = b_0 + b_1\times Less\)
  3. \(\widehat{gpa} = b_0 + b_1\times More\)
  4. \(\widehat{gpa} = b_0 + b_1\times More + b_2 \times Less\)
  5. \(\widehat{work} = b_0 + b_1\times gpa\)

Question 5

Using everything we have seen about the data so far (hint: refer back to question 2), what will the estimated values of \(b_1\) and \(b_0\) be?

You should only need information from questions prior to this one. Information in Question 7 may help you, but you should NOT rely on this information; there is no guarantee you would have it on the exam. I would recommend picking an answer to this question before going any further.

  1. \(b_0 = 3.576\), \(b_1 = 3.658\)
  2. \(b_0 = 3.658\), \(b_1 = 3.576\)
  3. \(b_0 = 3.576\), \(b_1 = 0.0814\)
  4. \(b_0 = 3.658\), \(b_1 = -0.0814\)
  5. \(b_0 = 3.658\), \(b_1 = 0.0814\)

Question 6

  2. \(H_0: \beta_0 \neq 0\), $H_A: _0 = 0 $
  3. \(H_0: \beta_1 = 0\), $H_A: _1 $
  4. \(H_0: \beta_1 \neq 0\), $H_A: _1 = 0 $

Part 2 - Blizzard

In 2020, employees of Blizzard Entertainment circulated a spreadsheet to anonymously share salaries and recent pay increases amidst rising tension in the video game industry over wage disparities and executive compensation. (Source: Blizzard Workers Share Salaries in Revolt Over Pay)

The name of the data frame used for this analysis is blizzard_salary and the variables are:

  • percent_incr: Raise given in July 2020, as percent increase with values ranging from 1 (1% increase to 21.5 (21.5% increase)

  • salary_type: Type of salary, with levels Hourly and Salaried

  • annual_salary: Annual salary, in USD, with values ranging from $50,939 to $216,856.

  1. 95% of all teens who text send between 1450 and 1550 text messages per month.
  2. If a new survey with the same sample size were to be taken, there is a 95% chance that the mean number of texts in the sample would be between 1450 and 1550.
